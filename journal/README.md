# Journal Title

Decoding the Thermostat
By: Jordan Freeman


The first time I tried to use my thermostat I was very confused.  To set the stage, It was one of those cold winter nights fifty degrees, maybe even less and my blanket
was not keeping me warm so I tried to change the thermostat to warm up the apartment.  A few initial observations I made were that there was an on/off switch indicating whether the thermostat was even affecting the temperature, giving immediate **feedback** (an action displayed by technology when something changes internally) by switching on a little green light. There was also a red up arrow and a blue down arrow, each with an **affordance** (the characteristics of a design that indicate its use) suggesting they would adjust the temperature when used.  However, the first action that I did was to press the mode button as initially it was set to cool, and when interacted with it changed to heat which I assumed would change the temperature of the air that was being produced, but after thirty of the thermostat being on the temperature didn't change at all.  It was only when I went back and changed the thermostat to seventy-one degrees that it started to warm up.  After that I was curious what actually affected the temperature and it turned out that the heat/cool setting does not affect the outcoming air regardless of the set temperature.  For instance seventy-one on heat and seventy-one on cool produced the same level of temperature, it was only when adjusting the fan speed and temperature that the outflow actually changed.
